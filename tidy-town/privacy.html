Privacy Notice

Tidy Town (“Tidy Town: Pack & Restore” on Google Play, “Tidy Town: Furniture Jam” on the App Store)

Updated October 3, 2026 · DizzyWalk Games (DIZZYWALK RECORDS LLC) · App identifier com.dizzywalk.tidytown

Tidy Town stores puzzle progress locally, shows ads (banners, interstitials, app-open and optional rewarded ads), offers optional purchases, and uses gameplay measurement (including session replay of new players’ first week) and advertising measurement subject to your privacy choices and applicable regional requirements. Purchases are handled by Google Play on Android and, where the iOS version is available, by the Apple App Store.

What we do not collect

Tidy Town does not ask for your name, email address, phone number, physical address, contacts, precise location, or payment-card or bank credentials. The platform store, Google Play or Apple, handles payment credentials for purchases.

Identifiers and local game data

The game uses a pseudonymous player ID for local game state and purchase verification. When optional analytics is enabled, it also creates a separate random telemetry identifier to associate gameplay and measurement events. Turning analytics off deletes that local telemetry identifier; turning it on again creates a new one. Core puzzle progress and restoration choices can run locally on your device. On iOS, any access to the advertising identifier or tracking that requires Apple permission also follows the system tracking permission; declining it does not prevent core gameplay.

Optional analytics, advertising, and attribution

Where the game requires a privacy choice (the EEA, the UK and Switzerland), optional analytics and attribution wait for your answer. In other regions, measurement is enabled from the first launch and can be turned off in Settings. Declining optional measurement does not prevent you from playing or verifying purchases. Ads may still be shown with privacy restrictions, subject to the advertising provider's consent requirements and ad availability.

When measurement is enabled, gameplay events, app version, technical signals, pseudonymous identifiers, and verified purchase or ad-revenue events may be sent through our telemetry service to PostHog and to Google Analytics for Firebase. We use these events to understand game performance, progression, purchases, and advertising results.

Google Analytics for Firebase. When measurement is enabled, the Firebase SDK also collects some information on its own: an app-instance ID that Firebase creates for this installation, device model, operating system and version, language, app version, and session and engagement information through automatic events such as first open, session start, engagement time, app updates and in-app purchases. Google Analytics estimates an approximate location (country, region and city) from your IP address. On Android, Firebase can read the Google advertising ID; on iOS it can read the identifier for vendor and, only if you allow tracking in Apple’s prompt, the advertising identifier (IDFA). The game sends Google’s consent-mode signals to Firebase. analytics_storage is granted only while measurement is enabled. ad_storage, ad_user_data and ad_personalization decide whether Google may use this data for advertising: in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland they follow your answer to Google’s consent message; elsewhere they are granted unless you turn off ANALYTICS & ATTRIBUTION in Settings; on iOS all three stay denied unless you also allow tracking. When they are granted, Google may use the data to measure advertising, including crediting installs and purchases to Google ads, and to personalize ads. Our Google Analytics property keeps event-level data for 2 months and user-level data for 14 months after the last activity; aggregated reports that do not identify you may be kept longer. Learn how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services, and see Firebase privacy information.

Tenjin (attribution). Tenjin provides install and campaign attribution. To match an install to an ad, the Tenjin SDK collects the advertising identifier (the Google advertising ID on Android; on iOS the IDFA only if you allow tracking), the Android app set ID or the iOS identifier for vendor, IP address, device model, operating system and version, language, app version, and the attribution events the game sends: install, early gameplay and level outcomes, verified purchases and subscriptions with their store receipts, and ad-revenue events. On iOS, if you have not allowed tracking, the game asks Tenjin not to collect the advertising identifier or IP address. Apple’s SKAdNetwork install reports, which carry no device identifier, are also sent to Tenjin. Where permitted by your choices and the enabled integration, Tenjin shares install and gameplay outcomes or verified purchase information with advertising partners including Meta and AppLovin. Meta may also receive separate ad-revenue measurement. Google measurement primarily uses Google Analytics for Firebase; Tenjin also forwards install events to Google. The enabled event types differ by partner. Purchase amounts and currencies are used for revenue measurement; game coins and other noncash rewards are not reported as purchases. Turning off ANALYTICS & ATTRIBUTION opts the game out of Tenjin. See Tenjin’s privacy policy.

Ads and advertising partners. AppLovin MAX mediates rewarded ads, interstitials, banners, and app-open ads, and chooses which network fills each ad request. The ad networks whose software is included in the game, and which may receive ad requests and serve ads through AppLovin MAX, are: AppLovin; Google AdMob; Meta Audience Network; Unity Ads and ironSource Ads (Unity Technologies); Liftoff Monetize (Vungle); Mintegral; Moloco; InMobi; Pangle (ByteDance); DT Exchange (Digital Turbine); and, on Android only, BidMachine and Bigo Ads. When an ad is requested or shown, AppLovin and the networks taking part may process the advertising identifier (on iOS only if you allow tracking), IP address, device and operating-system details, network information, app information, ad interactions, and device or app performance signals for ad delivery, frequency capping, fraud prevention, and measurement, and, where your choices allow, to personalize ads. Each network handles that data under its own privacy policy, linked above. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google’s consent message records your advertising choices and the ad SDKs read them. On Android, turning off ANALYTICS & ATTRIBUTION also tells AppLovin MAX that you have not consented and do not want your data sold or shared, and MAX passes that to the networks. On iOS, the system tracking permission controls whether the networks can use the advertising identifier. Ad revenue is measured separately from in-app purchases. PostHog’s privacy information is at PostHog.

You can change optional measurement choices in Settings. Turning measurement off clears the local telemetry queue and identifier and stops optional analytics collection; applicable SDK privacy or opt-out signals are updated. Changes do not retrospectively erase records already received by a service. Advertising providers may present additional privacy choices where required.

When optional telemetry is enabled, Sentry receives crash/error diagnostics, technical app and device context, and session/release-health information to help us identify reliability problems. This integration starts only when that permission is enabled and stops when it is declined. It does not attach screenshots, view hierarchies or default personal-data fields, and performance tracing is disabled. No custom player identity is assigned by the game’s crash-reporting wrapper. Network requests still reveal connection information to the provider. See Sentry’s privacy information. Turning reporting off does not erase reports already received by Sentry.

Session replay (UXCam). To find out where the game is confusing or broken, we record play sessions of new players during their first week after installing with UXCam (UXCam Inc.), a service that processes this data on our behalf. A recording shows the game’s own screens, where you tapped, which screens you visited and a few game events (such as starting or finishing a level, an offer or ad being shown, or a purchase being completed), together with your device model, operating system, app version, network type and random install IDs (the same random analytics ID described above; no name, email or account). UXCam estimates your country and city from your IP address and does not store the IP address. Recordings never include anything you type (text fields are masked), ads, Google Play or Apple purchase screens, or anything outside the game, and UXCam’s crash reporting is not used. Recording follows the same privacy choice as our analytics: in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland we record only after you agree, and everywhere you can turn analytics off in Settings, which stops recording and ends the current session. UXCam stores this data in the United States and keeps it for the retention period of our UXCam plan (UXCam’s documented default is 90 days), after which it is permanently deleted. To ask for your recordings to be deleted, contact us at the address below. See UXCam’s privacy policy.

Purchases and subscription records

For a purchase or restoration, the game sends store purchase proof, the product SKU, player ID, and install ID to its receipt-verification service. Purchase proof is a Google Play purchase token on Android or an Apple-signed transaction and associated purchase identifiers on iOS. On iOS, Apple's signed app transaction also supplies a store-scoped identifier used to recognize the same purchase owner when restoring eligible purchases; we do not receive your Apple Account password, name, or email address. These verification records are needed even when optional analytics and ads are off. The service retains purchase identifiers and proof hashes together with order, grant, and history records to support entitlement verification, restoration, subscription renewal, and refunds.

Purchase records may include the product, order identifier, verified amount and currency, entitlement, subscription renewal, and refund or revocation status. We use these records to deliver and restore purchases, reconcile charges and refunds, and prevent duplicate grants. These essential purchase records are separate from optional advertising measurement.

Children

Tidy Town is a casual puzzle game for a general audience of teens and adults. Its content ratings (Everyone on Google Play, 4+ on the App Store) describe the content, not the intended audience; the game is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13, or under the minimum age that applies where they live. If you believe a child has played the game and data about them was collected, contact us at the address below so we can delete the records we can link to that device.

Local removal, records, and requests

Clearing app storage or uninstalling the game removes local app data from that device. If Android backup is enabled, Android may restore progress, settings, your privacy choice, and saved identifiers when you reinstall or move to another device. The game excludes its per-device session record and its own unsent telemetry file from Android backup; this does not describe every third-party SDK's storage.

On iOS, deleting the app removes its local app data from that device. If iCloud Backup or a computer backup is on, iOS may include the game’s progress, settings, your privacy choice, and saved identifiers in the backup and restore them when you restore that backup to the same or a new device. The game also keeps a random install identifier in the iOS Keychain to recognize the original install for purchase delivery; it is limited to that device, is not synced through iCloud Keychain, and may remain on the device after the app is deleted.

Local removal does not delete telemetry or purchase-verification records already sent to server services. Those records may be retained to support access, restoration, refunds, and fraud prevention. Contact us about access or deletion requests using the address below.

Your privacy choices and rights

Choices in the game and on your device. Settings > ANALYTICS & ATTRIBUTION turns optional analytics, attribution, session replay and crash reporting on or off, together with the advertising signals described above. Where Google’s consent message applies, the AD PRIVACY / RETRY button in Settings opens it again. You can also reset or delete your advertising identifier in your device settings (Android: Settings > Google > Ads; iOS: Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking controls whether apps may ask to track you).

EEA, UK and Switzerland. Optional analytics, attribution, session replay, crash reporting and personalized advertising run only with your consent, which you can withdraw at any time in Settings. Purchase-verification records are processed to deliver and restore what you bought and are kept for accounting, refunds and fraud prevention. You can ask us for access to, correction, deletion or a copy of your personal data, and object to or ask us to restrict processing. You can also complain to the data protection authority where you live or work. Our service providers may process data in the United States and other countries.

United States. Depending on the state you live in, you may have the right to know about, access, correct and delete personal information, and to opt out of its “sale” or “sharing” and of targeted advertising. We do not sell personal information for money. Letting advertising and attribution partners use advertising identifiers and app activity to deliver, measure and personalize ads may count as a “sale” or “sharing” under some state laws. To opt out, turn off ANALYTICS & ATTRIBUTION in Settings and limit or reset your device advertising identifier as described above. We will not treat you differently for using these rights.

To make a request, email us at the address below. Because the game does not collect your name or email address, we may ask for details that let us find the records linked to your device or purchases.
