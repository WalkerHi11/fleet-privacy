Summary

  • SCRAPWARD does not ask for an email or password. It creates an anonymous purchase account to verify Google Play purchases. Campaign progress and your game wallet are saved on your device and backed up to our game server under that anonymous account (see Campaign backup).
  • We never ask for your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or precise location.
  • The game shows ads through AppLovin MAX. AppLovin and the ad partners that bid through it use your device’s advertising ID and device data to show and measure ads.
  • We use PostHog for gameplay analytics under a random install identifier, Tenjin for campaign attribution, Google Analytics for Firebase for purchase and ad measurement, Sentry for crash diagnostics, and UXCam to record how the game’s screens are used (session replay; not in the EEA, UK or Switzerland).
  • Purchases go through Google Play Billing. We never receive your card or bank details.
  • In the EEA, UK and Switzerland, Google’s consent form asks before ads are personalized. Everywhere, Settings → PRIVACY CHOICES lets you opt out of personalized ads and of the sharing described below.

Data kept on your device

The game stores on your device: your progress, currencies and items, purchase records (Google Play product IDs and order/transaction IDs, so a purchase is never granted twice), subscription status, ad frequency counters, your privacy and consent choices, notification settings, and your sound, vibration, text-size, colour-vision and language settings.

Campaign backup. A copy of this save (your progress, currencies, items, purchase records and settings, with an integrity check) is uploaded over HTTPS to our game server on Render, under your anonymous purchase account, after meaningful events such as an expedition result, a purchase or a claim. Each copy is stamped with the game version and your install identifier. It never contains your recovery code, session credential, purchase tokens or payment details. On a new installation, or after you recover your purchase account, the game can restore the backup or ask which copy to keep. The backup does not grant purchases; Google Play rechecks those. It can be turned off remotely, and it is deleted with your purchase account.

Install identifier. On first launch the game creates a random identifier for this installation. It is not derived from your advertising ID, phone number, email or any hardware identifier. We use it to link analytics events from the same installation, fetch configuration, and assign test groups for individual game settings. Clearing the game’s storage or reinstalling creates a new identifier.

Analytics and remote configuration (PostHog)

We use PostHog, Inc. as our analytics service provider. The game sends events over HTTPS to PostHog’s United States cloud (us.i.posthog.com). Analytics runs for every player of the released game; there is no in-app switch to turn it off. Each event carries:

  • the random install identifier and a random per-session identifier;
  • build details: game version and build number, platform, your in-game language setting and a coarse device memory tier (low, mid or high);
  • a flag marking internal test traffic and whether the installation is in the ads test group;
  • play and purchase context: highest mission reached, days since install, session number, whether the installation has paid or watched a rewarded ad, and the acquisition source (organic, paid or IAP campaign) and ad network reported by Tenjin.

The events describe how the game is played and how its store and ads perform: first launch, sessions, the tutorial, expedition starts and results (outcome, duration, score, mission), level-ups and perk choices, missions, login and event rewards, in-game currency spending, offers shown, tapped or dismissed, purchase attempts, completions, failures and restores (the product ID and the game’s list price, never your payment details), subscription starts, renewals and cancellations, rewarded-ad offers and completions, ad impressions and the ad revenue AppLovin reports for each one, your consent and privacy choices, notification permission answers and reminder opens, and the result of the configuration check, and the country your device reports (SIM, mobile network or language setting), which decides whether randomized items can be sold in your country.

We do not create PostHog person profiles, and we do not send your advertising ID, name, email or payment details to PostHog. Like any internet service, PostHog receives your IP address with each request and may use it to estimate an approximate location (such as country), according to our project settings.

Remote configuration. At launch the game asks PostHog’s feature-flag service for the game’s current configuration, sending the install identifier. The answer can adjust game tuning, offers, events, ad settings and ad frequency limits (always within fixed limits built into the game). It is saved on your device.

Crash diagnostics

Short error reports go to PostHog. We also use Sentry for managed and native crashes, stack traces, release-health sessions, app/build version and device/operating-system diagnostics. Sentry screenshot and view-hierarchy attachments and default personal-information collection are disabled. The service still receives ordinary network request information to deliver its service. See Sentry’s privacy policy. Google Play and Android may also collect crash and performance data under their own settings and policies.

Session replay (UXCam)

We use UXCam Inc. as a service provider to understand how players use the game’s screens, so we can fix confusing steps, broken flows and store or ad screens that do not work. When it runs, UXCam’s SDK records a video-like replay of the game screen (screenshots of what the game draws) together with your taps and gestures, the names of the game screens you visit, the gameplay and store events listed under Analytics (by the same names), session start, length and order, and device information such as model, operating system, screen size, language and app version. It is linked to the same random install identifier PostHog uses (never your name, email, advertising ID or payment details), with the properties listed for PostHog (for example payer yes/no, highest mission band, test group, internal-test flag). The replay link of each recorded session is added to our PostHog events. UXCam receives your IP address with each request and uses it to estimate your country and city; UXCam states that it does not store the IP address.

What is not recorded: the screen while any full-screen ad, the Google Play purchase sheet or another app is in front of the game; the on-screen keyboard; any text field; and the Settings, Restore Purchases, backup and account-deletion screens, which are hidden in the recording because they show your install or purchase-account IDs. UXCam’s crash reporting is off (crashes go to Sentry).

Who is recorded: session replay runs for a share of installations that we set remotely (currently all new and existing installations), during the first days after install (currently 7), and can be turned off remotely. It does not run in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland; on a device whose time zone or language suggests one of those countries it waits until the ad service confirms the region. Data is sent over HTTPS to UXCam’s United States region and kept for as long as our UXCam plan retains it, then deleted. See UXCam’s privacy policy.

Advertising (AppLovin MAX)

SCRAPWARD shows optional rewarded ads, which you choose to watch for the stated reward, interstitial ads at natural breaks after the tutorial, banners and app-open ads. Interstitials can appear in your first session after the tutorial and are not shown during an expedition. VIP PASS FOREVER turns off forced ads (interstitial, app-open and banner) permanently; VIP PASS 30 DAYS and the Salvage Pack + VIP PASS 30 Days turn them off for 30 days on the device where they are bought (kept in the local save, not restored after a reinstall). Rewarded ads stay optional. The Salvage Pass subscription does not remove ads. Delivery also follows the game’s payer and acquisition-source settings.

Ads are served by the AppLovin MAX software development kit (Android SDK 13.6.4) from AppLovin Corporation. AppLovin runs an auction in which AppLovin’s own demand and other ad partners (listed below) can bid to fill each ad. The game declares Android’s advertising ID permission. When the ad SDK runs, AppLovin and the ad partners that serve an ad may collect your device’s advertising ID and similar identifiers, device and network information (such as device model, operating system, language, carrier, connection type, IP address and the approximate location derived from it), and your ad interactions (which ads you see, how long you watch, whether you close, skip, complete or tap an ad, and installs that follow). Ad interactions are collected and shared with these ad partners for advertising, analytics (ad measurement), and fraud prevention, security and compliance. They use it to deliver, personalize and measure ads, to limit how often you see an ad, and to prevent fraud, under their own privacy policies (AppLovin privacy policy). The game passes your consent and opt-out choices to AppLovin, which relays them to the bidding partners. If you opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information in Google’s US state privacy message, the game sets AppLovin’s “Do Not Sell” signal before the ad SDK starts.

User ID sent to AppLovin. The game gives AppLovin the random install identifier described under Analytics as its user ID, so that AppLovin’s ad-revenue reports can be matched to this installation’s analytics. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland it is set only if you consent to personalized ads, and it is removed when you decline or withdraw that consent.

Google’s consent form is shown where required, through AppLovin MAX and Google UMP. Outside regions requiring that form, ads and measurement start at boot. Where consent is required, measurement follows the stored consent decision; declining personalized ads still permits limited ads where the consent framework allows them.

Attribution and revenue measurement (Tenjin)

We use Tenjin, Inc. for install attribution and for measuring purchase and ad revenue, so we can tell which ad campaigns bring players and whether they pay for themselves. Tenjin starts only after your consent decision (see Your choices) and receives nothing while you have opted out. When it runs, Tenjin’s SDK may collect:

  • your advertising ID and Android App Set ID, IP address, and device information such as model, operating system, language, carrier, connection type and screen size;
  • the Google Play install referrer, and the Meta install referrer where Facebook or Instagram is installed, to see which ad led to the install;
  • app opens and sessions;
  • for each verified purchase: the product ID, the price charged and currency, the Google Play order ID and the Google Play purchase receipt (purchase data and signature), which Tenjin uses to check the purchase is genuine;
  • for each ad shown: the ad revenue AppLovin reports, the ad network, format, ad unit, placement and country.

Tenjin may forward attributed installs, purchases and ad revenue to the advertising networks we buy ads from (for example AppLovin, Google and Meta) so they can measure and optimize our campaigns. See Tenjin’s privacy policy. A PostHog attribution row contains a one-way order-ID hash instead of the order ID.

Google Analytics for Firebase collects app-instance/device identifiers, app/device information and app interactions, automatic Google Play purchase/subscription events, and the ad-impression value reported by MAX. The studio’s Google Analytics property is linked to Google Play and Google Ads for revenue measurement and campaign optimization. Consent-mode signals control analytics storage, ad storage, ad-user-data use and personalization. Internal test installs are excluded from this production collection. See Google’s privacy policy.

Purchases, subscriptions and randomized items

Optional in-app purchases are processed by Google Play Billing. Google handles your payment method and billing details under the Google privacy policy; we never receive your card or bank details. The game checks the signed receipt and sends the product ID, purchase token and anonymous purchase-account identity to our verification service on Render. That service checks purchase status, account binding and subscription expiry with Google. Its ledger stores the account ID, product ID, order ID, status, verification times and a one-way token hash to prevent duplicate or cross-account grants. The raw token is used for the Google verification request; it is not saved in that ledger. The game stores delivery records and subscription status on your device. We also receive purchase and revenue reports in the Google Play Console.

Purchase-account recovery. A random private recovery code is enrolled for your anonymous purchase account. Our service stores its hash; the private code and session credential remain on your device. You can explicitly copy the code in Settings → RESTORE PURCHASES and later paste it there to recover that purchase account. Keep the code private: someone who has it can recover the account. Recovering the account lets the game offer that account’s latest campaign backup; it restores progress only as it was in that backup. Google Play rechecks owned permanent purchases and active subscriptions; the same Play account is still required.

The Salvage Pass is an auto-renewing monthly subscription billed by Google Play. The game reads its status (active, renewing, cancelled or expired) from Google Play to grant its benefits; you manage or cancel it in Google Play.

Some items, such as Elite Crates, Supply Crates and Gem Chests, contain randomly selected rewards. Their odds are shown in the game, and Elite Crates have two guarantees (“hard pity”): Epic or better within 5 crates and Legendary within 50. The counts toward them are kept in your local save.

Notifications

The game can send local reminders (for example when the Salvage Yard is full, a daily supply is ready, or an event is ending) and remote reminders through Firebase Cloud Messaging. Local reminders are scheduled on your device. After your first expedition win, when notifications are enabled and Android permission is granted, Firebase creates a notification registration token. Our game server associates that token with your purchase account and installation, language, time-zone offset and app version so it can deliver reminders. The server stores registration tokens encrypted; they are not included in gameplay analytics or game saves. On Android 13 and later, the game asks for notification permission only after your first expedition win, and asks at most twice. You can turn reminders off in Settings → NOTIFICATIONS or in Android settings. The game then removes its server registration and requests deletion of the Firebase token; an offline device retries when it reconnects. Account recovery also removes the previous registration before enrolling the recovered account. The game records permission status and which reminder opened it as analytics events. See Firebase’s privacy information.

Your choices

  • EEA, UK and Switzerland. Google’s consent form (Google User Messaging Platform, run through AppLovin MAX’s Terms & Privacy flow) asks whether ads may be personalized and whether ad data may be used for measurement. Your choice is stored on your device in the standard IAB consent format and passed to AppLovin and its partners. Ads are personalized, and Tenjin runs, only if you consent. If the form cannot be shown or you make no choice, the game treats it as “no”.
  • Everywhere else. Ads may be personalized and Tenjin measurement runs by default. No prompt is shown; you can opt out at any time.
  • Settings → PRIVACY CHOICES. This row appears once the ad service has started (before that, no advertising data is shared). In the EEA, UK and Switzerland it reopens Google’s consent form. Elsewhere it lets you OPT OUT of personalized ads and sharing: the game tells AppLovin you do not consent to personalized ads and sets its “Do Not Sell” signal, and stops sending data to Tenjin (and, if enabled, to Google or Meta measurement). You can opt back in from the same row.
  • Advertising ID. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads (the path varies by device).
  • Delete local data. Android Settings → Apps → SCRAPWARD → Storage → Clear storage, or uninstall the game. This deletes your progress, settings, choices and the install identifier.

After opting out, you still see ads, but they are not personalized. Non-personalized ads may still use device and network data for delivery, frequency limits, measurement and fraud prevention. Analytics with PostHog continues, because PostHog processes data only on our behalf.

  • Advertising partners: AppLovin and the ad partners listed above, for their own advertising, measurement and fraud-prevention purposes as well as ours.

  • Others when required: where the law requires it, to protect rights, safety or security, or as part of a merger, acquisition or sale of the game, in which case this policy continues to apply.

We do not sell your data for money. Sharing with advertising and attribution partners may count as “selling” or “sharing” under some U.S. state laws; see U.S. state privacy rights.

Legal bases (EEA, UK and Switzerland)

  • Consent: personalized advertising and attribution measurement with Tenjin, and notification permission. You can withdraw consent at any time in Settings → PRIVACY CHOICES.
  • Contract: processing your purchases and subscription and delivering what you bought.
  • Legitimate interests: gameplay analytics, remote configuration, error reports, serving non-personalized ads that fund the free game, frequency limits, security and fraud prevention. You can object by contacting us.

Retention

Local data stays on your device until you clear it or uninstall the game. We keep analytics events in PostHog for as long as we need them to run and improve the game, and delete them when they are no longer needed or when the retention settings of our PostHog project expire them. UXCam session recordings are kept for the retention period of our UXCam plan and then deleted. The campaign backup holds only the latest copy and is kept while your purchase account exists; it is deleted with the account. AppLovin, the ad partners, Tenjin and Google keep data under their own retention policies. Purchase and revenue records in the Google Play Console are kept as long as tax and accounting law requires.

Access and deletion requests

To ask for a copy of your data, email michael@dizzywalk.com. To initiate deletion of your purchase account and associated server data (including its campaign backup), use Settings → RESTORE PURCHASES → REQUEST ACCOUNT DELETION or our public account deletion request page. The page works without signing in or connecting the game's account service. It opens an email draft for you to send to michael@dizzywalk.com; requests are reviewed and fulfilled manually.

In the game, COPY REQUEST IDENTIFIERS copies your non-secret purchase account ID and install ID for support. These help locate records but are not, by themselves, proof of ownership. If unavailable, you can still initiate a request. We may ask for an approximate installation date or device model to locate records and verify the request. If you made a purchase, a Google Play order ID is optional. Never email your private SWR recovery code, authentication tokens, payment details or full receipt. We remove eligible matching data we hold and request removal from our service providers where applicable, including UXCam session recordings linked to your install ID. Necessary transaction records may remain for tax and accounting obligations, fraud prevention or legal disputes, only as needed for those purposes.

Clearing Android storage or uninstalling removes the local save; progress can come back only from your campaign backup, through the same purchase account or its recovery code. It does not itself delete server records. A server deletion request does not erase the device's local save or cancel or refund a Google Play subscription. Manage subscriptions separately in Google Play. To request removal of data held independently by ad partners, use the contact links in their policies above.

International transfers

We and our partners process data in the United States and other countries whose data-protection laws may differ from yours. PostHog processes our analytics in the United States, and UXCam stores session replays in its United States region. Where we transfer personal data out of the EEA, UK or Switzerland, we rely on the partner’s adequacy status (for example certification under the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework and its UK and Swiss extensions) or on the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses and the UK Addendum.

EEA, UK and Swiss rights

You have the right to access, correct, delete or port your personal data, to restrict or object to its use, and to withdraw consent at any time (without affecting earlier processing). You also have the right to complain to your data-protection authority. To use these rights, email us. Dizzywalk is the controller of the data described in this policy that the game sends to us; our partners are independent controllers of the data they collect for their own purposes.

Children

SCRAPWARD is not directed at children under 13 and is not part of Google Play’s Families program. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game, contact us and we will delete the related data we hold.

Security

The game sends data only over encrypted connections (HTTPS). The local save carries an integrity check. No method of transmission or storage is perfectly secure, but we limit what we collect to what the game needs.

Earlier development test accounts

Earlier development builds used optional game-service accounts and recovery snapshots. The current Android game uses that service for anonymous purchase identity, receipt verification and the campaign backup described above. Historical recovery snapshots follow their existing deletion schedule. When you open this page, the web host processes standard request data, such as your IP address, to deliver it.

Changes

We will update this policy before the game adds new kinds of data collection, new recipients, accounts or cloud saves, and we will change the effective date above. Where the law requires, we will ask for your consent again.
