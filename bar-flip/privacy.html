What changed on 2026-09-15, and why.

BAR FLIP has been rebuilt. Earlier versions of the app — up to and including version code 200 — transmitted nothing off your device, and the previous version of this page said so correctly. The rebuilt app can send anonymous gameplay analytics, but only if you turn them on: it asks on the first screen, and it sends nothing at all until you answer. This page has been rewritten to describe the rebuilt app honestly rather than edited around the change.

If you are still running an older version, nothing has started leaving your device because this page changed. The behaviour described below arrives with the update.

This Privacy Policy explains how Dizzywalk ("we", "us") handles information in connection with the game BAR FLIP (the "Game", package com.dizzywalk.barflip) on Google Play and related platforms. BAR FLIP is a rhyme-duel word game: a rival throws you a word and you swipe letters off a wheel to spell one that rhymes. This page describes exactly what the Game does and does not collect, and it was written by reading the shipping build rather than by describing an intention.

Short version.
  • No account and no login — there is nothing to sign up for.
  • Analytics are off until you say yes. The Game asks on the first screen and sends nothing before you answer. You can change the answer at any time in Settings.
  • If you say yes, the Game sends anonymous gameplay events — which screens you opened, which shows you started and finished, how long a word was — and crash reports.
  • It never sends the words you play. It sends how many letters a word had, never the word.
  • No name, email, contacts, photos, location, or advertising identifier — ever, on any setting.
  • No advertising and no in-app purchases in this version.
  • We do not sell your data.

The choice you make on the first screen

The first time you open the Game it explains what it would like to record and gives you a switch. Until you answer, the Game installs no network reporting and sends nothing — not a screen view, not an app-open, nothing. A switch sitting in its default position is not an answer, and the Game does not treat it as one. The disclosure comes before any collection.

If you answer no, no reporting is created at all: there is nothing to disable later because nothing was ever started. If you answer yes, you can withdraw at any time from Settings → Share anonymous play data. Withdrawing stops collection and discards anything the Game was still holding to send.

What we collect if you say yes

WhatExamples
Gameplay eventsthe app was opened; a show was started, won or lost; a screen was opened; a daily was claimed; a bar was submitted, whether it scored, and how many letters it had
An anonymous identifiera random id the Game generates on your device the first time it runs, so that two events from the same install can be counted as one session. It is not your device's advertising id, it is not derived from any hardware identifier, and it is not joined to a person
App and session contextapp version, platform, a per-run session id, which launch this is for this install, and how many days since the install first opened
Crash reportsdiagnostics about a crash or error, so we can fix it. These follow the same switch: they are armed when you opt in and cleared when you opt out

That is the whole list. The Game keeps a written map of every event and every property it is allowed to send, and an automated check compares that map against the source code in both directions, so an event the map does not describe fails the build.

The words you play

BAR FLIP is played by swiping letters off a wheel to spell words. Every word is judged on your device against a bundled pronunciation dictionary — nothing you spell is sent anywhere to be judged or scored. The words themselves are never sent to us. When analytics are on, a submitted bar is reported as a length and a result — for example "8 letters, scored" — and never as text.

What we never collect

On any setting, the Game does not ask for or collect your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, camera, microphone, location, or any government identifier, and it does not use an advertising identifier. The Game has no account system and no login. The Android permission it declares is vibration; the standard network permission is used only for the analytics described above.

Who processes the data

If you opt in, events are sent over HTTPS to PostHog, the product- analytics service we use as our data processor, on its United States infrastructure (us.i.posthog.com). If you are outside the United States, this means the anonymous event data described above is transferred there. The Game embeds no third-party advertising, attribution, or analytics SDK — the reporting is the Game's own code posting to that service — and the Game is configured so that no personal profile is created on the analytics side. We do not share data with advertising networks or data brokers, and we do not sell your data.

Sharing a card

The Game has a card feature: you can copy a short code for the bars you played on a given day to your device's clipboard, and paste in a code someone sent you so your device can re-score their bars. That code contains the words that were played — that is how the receiving device can check them. It goes to your clipboard and nowhere else: there is no server behind this feature, we never see the code, and you choose entirely whether and where to paste it. A card carries no name and no profile.

Advertising

This version shows no advertising and contains no advertising or attribution SDK. If advertising is ever added, this page and the Play Data Safety declaration would be updated before that version ships.

Purchases

This version contains no in-app purchases and no billing integration — the shipping app offers nothing for sale and no transaction can be completed. If purchases are added in a future version, they would be processed by Google Play, we would never see or store your payment-card data, and this page would be updated before that version ships.

Other players

There is no leaderboard, no rank and no matchmaking, and the Game never presents a recorded or authored opponent as a live person. The rivals you face are written characters shipped inside the app.

AI-assisted development

Some of the Game's art and audio were created with the help of AI-assisted tools during development, all reviewed by a human before shipping. No AI model or large language model runs inside the shipped Game, and none is used to judge your words: rhymes are scored by a deterministic phoneme engine over the Carnegie Mellon Pronouncing Dictionary, so the same word in the same position always scores the same. Players cannot prompt the Game to generate content, and no personal information is used for any AI processing.

Children's privacy

BAR FLIP is a word game with a stylised stage-and-crowd setting, intended for teens and older; it is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided information to us, contact us and we will delete it.

Data retention and security

Your tour progress, gear, records and settings are stored only on your device and stay there until you clear the app's data or uninstall it (subject to your platform's own backup behaviour). If you opted in, the anonymous event data described above is held by our analytics processor under its own retention schedule and is not joined to a personal profile. Data is encrypted in transit. We honour Google Play's Data Safety declarations for the Game.

Your choices and rights

You can turn analytics off at any time in Settings → Share anonymous play data, which stops collection and discards anything still held to send. You can clear everything the Game has stored by clearing the app's data or uninstalling it. Because the identifier we use is anonymous and is not joined to a person, we generally cannot look up an individual's events from a name or an email — but if you contact us with the relevant details we will help as far as that identifier allows. Depending on where you live (for example under GDPR or the CCPA/CPRA), you may have rights to access, delete, or object to processing of information about you; contact us to exercise them.
