BAR FLIP — Privacy Policy

Published by Dizzywalk

Effective date: 2026-09-16 · Last updated: 2026-09-16

This Privacy Policy explains how Dizzywalk ("we", "us") handles information in connection with the game BAR FLIP (the "Game", package com.dizzywalk.barflip) on Google Play and related platforms. BAR FLIP is a rhyme-duel word game. This page describes what the Game does and does not collect, and the factual claims in it are checked against the shipping Android build by an automated test that fails when the two disagree.

Short version.
  • No login, no password, no email, and no third-party sign-in. The Game does create one thing that behaves like an account: a random recovery code, so a purchase can be restored after a reinstall.
  • The Game sends anonymous gameplay analytics to PostHog, only after you answer the question it asks before your first show, and you can turn it off at any time in Settings.
  • It offers in-app purchases through Google Play, and opt-in rewarded video ads through AppLovin. It asks for non-personalised ads for every player, everywhere.
  • Your daily runs, scores, streaks, profile and settings live on your device. The words you play are judged on your device and are never sent to us.
  • We do not sell your data.

The question we ask before your first show

The first screen in the Game, before you can play, asks whether you are willing to share anonymous play data. You cannot dismiss it with the back gesture; you have to answer it. Until you answer, nothing is transmitted — the handful of events the Game generates while it starts up are held in memory and are discarded if you decline.

The switch on that screen starts in the "on" position, so accepting without changing it opts you in. Your answer is stored on your device, and you can change it whenever you like in Settings → SHARE ANONYMOUS PLAY DATA. Turning it off stops delivery, empties the queue held in memory, and deletes the queue file on disk, so nothing that was waiting to be sent survives the decision.

Analytics

When you have opted in, the Game sends product analytics to PostHog (https://us.i.posthog.com), our data processor. There is no PostHog SDK inside the Game and no autocapture, session recording, or cookies: the Game builds its own batches and posts them itself, so what follows is the whole of what is sent.

What is in an event

Gameplay and interface facts, as numbers and short labels — which screen you opened, that a show started or ended, how many bars landed, how long a run took, which tactic you chose, that a purchase or a rewarded ad was started, completed or failed, and how far you have progressed. Every event carries a build identifier, the platform, a session identifier, how many sessions you have played, how many days it has been since you first opened the Game, and a distinct_id.

What is not in an event

Never the words you play. The Game reports the length of a submitted word and never the word itself. There is also no name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, precise location, government identifier, or advertising identifier in any analytics payload, and no field that carries free text you have entered. There is nowhere in the Game to type a name: your profile carries a fixed stage name the Game gives every player, which you cannot change and which is never sent anywhere.

The identifier

The distinct_id is a random identifier generated on your device when your profile is created. It is not your advertising ID, not an Android device ID, and not shared with any other app: it lives in the Game's own storage on your device — its save file, or, before a profile has been loaded, the app's own preferences — and reinstalling the Game or clearing its data produces a new one. We ask PostHog not to build a person profile against it. As with any internet request, PostHog observes the network address the request came from at the moment it arrives; we do not put your address in a payload and do not use it to locate you.

Where events wait

Events are queued in the Game's own private storage on your device (a file the OS removes when you uninstall) and sent in batches. The queue holds at most 500 events and drops the oldest when it is full. It is deleted outright if you withdraw consent.

Crash reports

The Game contains no third-party crash-reporting SDK — no Sentry, no Crashlytics, no Bugsnag, and Unity's own cloud diagnostics are switched off. Instead, when the Game hits an unhandled error it records a first-party report and sends it, under the same consent as analytics and only if you have opted in, through the same PostHog transport.

A report carries the error type, the exception message (clipped to 300 characters), the stack trace (clipped to 2,000 characters), whether it was fatal, whether the Game was in the foreground, and how long the session had been running. File paths are stripped out of the message and stack before sending, because on Android those paths embed your device account name. The Game reads only its own in-process errors and Android's own record of why the app last exited; it does not read the system log or crash dumps, and it asks for no permission to do either. A report that arrives before we know your consent answer is held in memory for a few seconds and then discarded — it is never written to disk and never sent.

The words you play

BAR FLIP is played by swiping across letters on a wheel to spell a word; there is no text box and nothing to type. Every word you spell is judged on your device against a pronunciation dictionary bundled inside the app — 56,064 entries derived from the Carnegie Mellon Pronouncing Dictionary. Nothing you spell is sent anywhere for judging, scoring, or any other purpose, and there is no network call anywhere in the path that decides whether a word is a word or whether it rhymes.

Sharing a duel card

If you choose to share a duel card, the Game puts a short code on your device's clipboard. You choose where to paste it, and the card itself is never uploaded: we never see it. If you have opted in to analytics, the Game records that a card was shared — the date, the score to beat, and how many characters the code ran to — never the code, and never the words inside it.

Be aware of what the card contains. So that another player can replay your show, the code encodes the bars you wrote. It carries no score — a reader recomputes that from the words — but the words themselves are in there, and it is not encrypted — anyone you give the code to, or anyone who gets it afterwards, can decode it and read those words. That is the feature working as intended, but it is the one place where words you played can leave your device, and it happens only when you copy a card and paste it somewhere yourself.

Purchases

The Game offers in-app purchases, processed by Google Play. There are seven items: a starter bundle, a tour pass, a consumables pack, and four sizes of gem pack. They are one-time or consumable purchases. There are no subscriptions and nothing renews automatically. There are no loot boxes, gacha pulls, or randomised rewards of any kind — every paid item grants a fixed, stated set of contents. Prices are always the ones Google Play reports for your country; the Game never displays a price of its own invention, and it does not vary prices between players. Where Google Play has no price for your country, the item is not offered at all, rather than shown at a price you could not be charged — so in some countries there is currently nothing in the Game to buy.

We never see or store your payment-card details. Google Play handles the payment and tells the Game that a purchase happened.

Checking a purchase

To make sure a purchase is genuine before the Game grants it, the Game sends the Google Play purchase token — and nothing else — to our purchase-verification service over HTTPS. The request carries your recovery code (described below) as its authorisation credential. The service asks Google whether the token is valid and returns a short signed statement naming the item and whether it was granted; the Game checks that signature against a key built into the app before it grants anything.

The verification request contains no name, email address, device identifier, advertising identifier, or analytics identifier. The signed statement is held in memory only and is never written to your save file. What the save file records is the transaction's identifier, so the Game does not grant the same purchase twice.

Your recovery code

The Game has no account system and no login. It does not ask for your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, or location, and there is no sign-in with Google, Facebook, or anyone else. You can play the entire Game without providing any personal information.

It does, however, create one identifier that works a little like an account, and you should know it exists. The first time you open the Game it generates a random 64-character recovery code and stores it in your save file. It is generated from your device's random number source — it is not derived from your device, your hardware, or anything about you, and it carries no name, address, or device identifier. Its purpose is narrow: it is the credential that lets our verification service confirm a purchase belongs to you, so that if you reinstall the Game or move to a new phone you can get back what you paid for.

The Game shows it to you after your first purchase and lets you copy it. Keep it somewhere safe; it is the only way to restore a purchase. A one-way hash of the code — not the code itself — is also handed to Google Play as the obfuscated account identifier attached to your purchases, which is how Play associates a transaction with a player without being told who the player is. The code itself is sent only to our verification service, only as the authorisation header on a purchase check, and nowhere else.

Advertising

The Game offers opt-in rewarded video ads through AppLovin MAX. A rewarded ad appears only where the Game offers you one in exchange for something — a second chance after a loss, a fresh letter rack, extra time on the clock, or doubled credits — and only if you choose to watch it. Rewarded video is the only ad format in the Game: there are no banners, no interstitials between screens, and no ads that open when you launch it.

AppLovin MAX chooses between several advertising networks to fill each rewarded ad, and their software is bundled with the Game. Besides AppLovin's own network they are Google AdMob, Meta Audience Network, Unity Ads and Vungle (Liftoff). Whichever one serves you an ad may read your device's advertising ID for that ad, on the same terms described below. Their privacy policies: AppLovin, Google, Meta, Unity, Liftoff.

The advertising ID, and what we tell the ad network

Because the advertising SDK is present, the app declares Android's advertising-ID permission (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID), and AppLovin may read your device's advertising ID to serve, cap, and measure an ad. You can reset that ID, or delete it entirely, in your Android settings under Privacy → Ads; the Game keeps working either way.

The Game does not ask the ad network to personalise anything. At startup, for every player, in every region, it tells the SDK that it does not have consent to use your data for personalised advertising, and that your personal information must not be sold or shared. The Game applies both signals before the advertising SDK starts, for every player and every region. The Game's own screens offer you no control over them, and nothing in this version changes them. The Game never sends your advertising ID to us, and it is not in any analytics or purchase payload.

You are told about this before you play. The first screen in the Game describes the advertising SDK and the advertising ID, alongside the analytics question, and the Game does not start the advertising SDK until you have dismissed that screen. Being told is not the same as being asked, and this page does not pretend otherwise: there is no choice on that screen about advertising, because there is no setting whose alternatives would produce a different signal.

Where consent is required, a separate form asks you

In regions whose law requires your consent before advertising may use your data — the EEA and the UK — you are shown a consent form provided by Google's User Messaging Platform, presented by our advertising partner before ads are requested. The Game does not draw that form, does not style it and does not store your answer; your choice is recorded by that platform and read by the advertising networks directly. It is a different thing from the first screen described above, which tells you what happens and asks you nothing about advertising.

Attribution and measurement

The Game does not measure which advertisement or campaign brought you to it. No attribution or mobile-measurement SDK is built into this release — there is no such library in the app, and the Game never creates an attribution client of any kind. You will not find a setting for this, because there is nothing to switch on. If that ever changes, we will update this page and the Play Data Safety declaration in the same release that changes it.

Reminders

The Game can send you reminders: that the day's new word is up, and that a daily streak is about to end. These are off by default. They turn on only when you switch on Settings → REMINDERS and grant Android's notification permission, and you can switch them off again in either place.

Reminders are scheduled by the Game on your device, by your device's own clock. There is no push service, no push token, and no Firebase or other messaging SDK in the app; a reminder itself never leaves your device. If you have opted in to analytics, switching reminders on or off is reported as one of the settings events described above — that is the only part of this feature that is ever sent.

Leaderboards and other players

There is no leaderboard, no rank, no league, and no percentile in the Game, and no server behind it that could hold one. No score of yours is ever submitted to a ranking, published, or shown to another player. If you have opted in to analytics, the score of a finished run is one of the numbers in the event that records the run ending, exactly as described under Analytics above — that is the only place it goes. The opponents you play against are not people and are not recordings of people: they are deterministic patterns the Game works out on your own device from the day's seed, and the Game never presents one as a live human. The one way to play against another person's run is the duel card described above, which travels by clipboard between the two of you and not through us.

Network access

The Game declares the standard Android INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE and ACCESS_WIFI_STATE permissions; the last of those comes with the advertising SDK. In this version they are used for exactly three things: delivering analytics and crash reports if you have opted in, checking a purchase with our verification service, and the advertising SDK's own requests when you choose to watch a rewarded ad. Nothing else in the Game makes a network request.

AI-assisted development

Some of the Game's art and audio were created with the help of AI-assisted tools during development, all reviewed by a human before shipping. No AI model or large language model runs inside the shipped Game, and none is used to judge your words: rhymes are scored by a deterministic phoneme engine over the Carnegie Mellon Pronouncing Dictionary, so the same word in the same position always scores the same. Players cannot prompt the Game to generate content, and no personal information is used for any AI processing.

Children's privacy

BAR FLIP is a word game with a stylised bar-room setting, intended for teens and older; it is not directed to children under 13, and it is not distributed in Google Play's programmes for families. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided us with personal information, contact us and we will delete it.

Who receives data

  • PostHog — product analytics and crash reports, only if you have opted in.
  • Our purchase-verification service — a Google Play purchase token and your recovery code, only when you make a purchase. It talks to Google's Play Developer API to confirm the purchase.
  • Google Play — processes your payment and receives a one-way hash of your recovery code as the obfuscated account identifier on a transaction.
  • AppLovin, and the advertising networks it mediates — Google AdMob, Meta Audience Network, Unity Ads and Vungle (Liftoff) — serve and measure rewarded video ads, and may read your device's advertising ID when you choose to watch one.

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it with data brokers. We do not share analytics data with advertising networks.

Data retention and security

On-device data — your progress, profile, settings, recovery code and queued analytics — lives only on your device, in the Game's own private storage, until you clear the app's data or uninstall it (subject to your platform's own backup behaviour). Analytics events wait on your device until they are delivered or dropped, and are deleted immediately if you withdraw consent. Transmitted data is sent over HTTPS. Analytics held by PostHog is retained under our agreement with them and is not tied to a name or an email address, because we never collect one. We honour Google Play's Data Safety declarations for the Game.

Your choices and rights

  • Analytics and crash reports — Settings → SHARE ANONYMOUS PLAY DATA. Turning it off stops delivery and deletes what was queued.
  • Reminders — Settings → REMINDERS, or Android's own notification settings.
  • Advertising ID — reset or delete it in Android's Privacy → Ads settings. Ads in the Game are non-personalised regardless.
  • Everything else — clearing the app's data or uninstalling the Game removes the on-device copy, including your recovery code. Save it first if you have made a purchase.

Because we hold no name, email address, or account, we generally cannot identify you in our analytics in order to find your records. Depending on where you live (for example under GDPR or the CCPA/CPRA), you may have rights to access, delete, or object to processing of information about you; contact us with your distinct_id or recovery code and we will do what we can to honour a request.
